I live in Ibaraki, Japan. On YouTube and TikTok I share real life in rural Japan — my work, overtime, food, and the nature around me.

Why I share Ibaraki

Ibaraki is often called Japan’s “least attractive” prefecture. Honestly, that makes me a little sad. Because here we have hills of flowers, a giant waterfall, the ocean and mountains — only about one hour from Tokyo. Most people just don’t know it yet.

So I want to change Ibaraki’s image, and show people all over the world how beautiful it is.

Getting here from Tokyo

easier than you think!
Everyone thinks Ibaraki is far. It's not! Pick your style 👇
🚄

Limited Express “Hitachi” / “Tokiwa”

Tokyo · Ueno · Shinagawa → Mito (JR Joban Line)
⏱ ~70–90 min💴 ~¥4,000Fastest

Comfy reserved seats. For Hitachi Seaside Park, get off at Katsuta (one stop after Mito) and take the bus. Covered by the JR Pass.

🚆

Tsukuba Express (TX)

Akihabara → Tsukuba
⏱ ~45 min (Rapid)💴 ~¥1,200For Mt. Tsukuba

From Tsukuba Station, take the shuttle bus to Mount Tsukuba. Great for a hiking day.

🚌

Highway bus

Tokyo Station (Yaesu South Exit) → Mito Station
⏱ ~2–2.5 h💴 ~¥2,250Cheapest

Takes longer, but saves money. Traffic can make it slower on weekends.

🚗

Rent a car

Tokyo → Joban Expressway
⏱ ~1.5–2 h to MitoBest for countryside

Fukuroda Falls, Ryujin Bridge and Hananuki Gorge are much easier by car. You need an International Driving Permit.

💡 Suica/PASMO cards work on JR trains and most buses. Local buses are not frequent — check times on Google Maps before you go. Times and prices are approximate.

Welcome

Hello from Ibaraki

Your friend in Ibaraki, Kaho

Hi, I'm Kaho. I live in Ibaraki — the prefecture that Japan keeps calling its “least attractive.”

But here's the secret: Ibaraki is just one hour from Tokyo, and it has quiet mountain shrines, a giant waterfall, apple orchards, a wild fish market, and the best local food I know.

This guide is not about famous tourist spots. It's about the places I really go — with my family, on my days off, after long workdays.

If you visit, post with #KahoIbaraki — I'd love to see it!

Before you go

Getting to Ibaraki

Closer than you think
  • Tokyo → Mito: JR Limited Express “Hitachi” or “Tokiwa” from Ueno / Shinagawa. About 70–80 min.
  • Akihabara → Tsukuba: Tsukuba Express. About 45 min.

More options (bus, car) are on the Home tab.

How to read this guide

🚃 Train OK Easy by train or on foot from the station.
🚃🚌 Train + bus/taxi Train, then a short bus or taxi ride.
🚗 Car needed Deep countryside. Almost no buses or taxis.

Kaho's honest tips

  • Trains in the countryside are not frequent. Check times before you go.
  • For the best spots, renting a car in Mito makes life much easier.
  • Many small shops take cash only. Bring yen!
  • Not much English outside the city — a smile and pointing works great.

Area 2 · Hitachinaka

Nakaminato Fish Market

Where locals go for the freshest seafood
🚃 Train OK

A busy fish market by the port, full of energy. Shop staff shout to sell fresh fish, crabs, oysters and shellfish — real Japan, not a tourist show.

  • Eat a kaisendon (seafood rice bowl)
  • Buy grilled seafood on a stick and eat it right there
  • Walk the port and see the fishing boats
  • Try fishing! 🎣 So relaxing, even for beginners
Kaho's tip: I caught 20 houbou here!
Houbou (red gurnard) has beautiful blue “wings” and little “legs” to walk on the sea floor. It even croaks when you catch it! Sometimes you can catch tai (sea bream) too — a lucky fish in Japan, because “tai” sounds like medetai, “congratulations.”
Access Hitachinaka Seaside Railway, Katsuta → Nakaminato Sta., then 10 min walk.
Go in the morning. Only fish where it's allowed.

Area 3 · Hitachi

Oiwa Shrine

The secret power spot locals don't tell tourists about
🚃🚌 Train + bus/taxi

Hidden deep in the mountains of Hitachi, Oiwa Shrine is one of the most sacred places in Ibaraki. More than 180 gods are worshipped here — very rare in Japan.

Walk through a quiet forest of huge cedar trees. Don't miss the Sanbon-sugi (Three Cedars), a giant tree hundreds of years old that splits into three trunks.

People say an astronaut once saw a strange light from this area from space. True or not, when you stand here, you'll feel something special.

Access JR Hitachi Station, then bus or taxi. Car is easiest.
Wear good shoes — the path to the top of Mt. Oiwa is a real mountain trail.

Area 4 · Daigo

Fukuroda Falls

One of Japan's three most beautiful waterfalls
🚃🚌 Train + bus/taxi

120 m tall and 73 m wide, the water falls down the rock in four steps — so it's called “Yodo no Taki,” the Four-Times Falls. A famous old poet said you must visit in all four seasons to see its true beauty.

🌱 Springfresh green
💧 Summercool mist
🍁 Autumnred & yellow leaves
❄️ Wintersometimes frozen ice falls
Access JR Suigun Line to Fukuroda Sta., then short bus/taxi. About 1 hr by car from Mito.
Small entrance fee for the tunnel to the viewing deck. Try grilled ayu fish on the street!
📍 Google Maps

Apple Picking

The “phantom apples” of Daigo
🚗 Car needed

In autumn, Daigo turns into apple country. Along the “Apple Road” (Ringo Kaido), family-run orchards let you pick apples yourself.

Daigo apples are not famous in Tokyo — most are sold only here, so locals call them “phantom apples.” Sweet, juicy, and fresh from the tree.

Kaho's favorite: Toyota Apple Farm (豊田りんご園)
📍 Google Maps
Season around September–November (check before you go)
You need a car. Rent one in Mito (about 1 hr), or combine with Fukuroda Falls on a road trip.

Area 5 · Tsukuba

Mt. Tsukuba Hike + Onsen

Climb in the morning, soak in the afternoon
🚃🚌 Train + bus/taxi

Mt. Tsukuba (877 m) is Ibaraki's famous twin-peaked mountain — Nantai-san and Nyotai-san. It's a sacred mountain, and on a clear day you can see the whole Kanto plain, even Tokyo far away.

  • Hike up: trails start near Tsukubasan Shrine. The Miyukigahara course is the classic one — steep in places, so take your time.
  • Too tired? Ride the cable car or the ropeway up or down. Hike up, ride down is a great combo.
  • Wear real shoes and bring water. The path has lots of rocks and roots.
Kaho's pick: hike, then onsen!
After climbing, go straight to a hot spring at the foot of the mountain. Your tired legs will thank you. Hiking + onsen = the perfect Japanese day.
Access Tsukuba Express from Akihabara to Tsukuba Sta. (about 45 min), then the shuttle bus to Tsukubasan Shrine.
Many hotels around the mountain offer day-trip onsen (higaeri onsen). Check hours before you go — and bring a small towel!
📍 Tsukubasan Shrine ♨️ Onsen near Mt. Tsukuba

🥐 Bakery Penny Lane (Tsukuba)

A big bakery café themed on the Beatles — the music, the decor, even some bread names! It started in Nasu (Tochigi), and the Tsukuba shop has a bakery, a restaurant and even a dog café.

Kaho's pick: Penny Lane!
Their famous one is the swirly Blueberry Bread. Perfect to grab before or after Mt. Tsukuba.

Festivals & Fireworks

Ibaraki's Big Days

📅 See the event calendar
Pottery fairs, summer festival and fireworks — Kaho's picks

🔥 Kasama Himatsuri (Pottery Fire Festival)

🚃🚌 Train + bus

Every Golden Week (April 29 – May 5), 200+ potters and kilns gather in Kasama Art Forest Park. Each shop is handmade by the potters themselves, and you can buy Kasama-yaki directly from the artists. The potters even run the food stalls!

When April 29 – May 5 every year · Where Kasama Art Forest Park
Small entry fee. Shuttle buses run from JR Tomobe / Kasama stations during the festival.

🍂 Autumn pottery fair in Kasama

In autumn, Kasama Art Forest Park has another big pottery market with local food too. Calmer than Golden Week — perfect for slowly finding your favorite cup.

Usually around October–November. Dates change every year — check the Kasama tourism site.
📍 Google Maps

🏮 Mito Komon Matsuri

🚃 Train OK

Mito's biggest summer festival, for Mito Komon (Tokugawa Mitsukuni). Drum floats, a glowing paper-lantern parade, a dance carnival, and one of Japan's biggest mikoshi (portable shrines) — right on the main street from Mito Station.

When early August (a Saturday & Sunday) · Where Mito Station North Exit area, free
Don't miss the finale when all the drum floats battle together!

🎆 Mito Kairakuen Fireworks

The festival starts with fireworks over Lake Senba in late July.

Kaho's pick: Japan's No.1 fireworks makers live in Mito!
The fireworks are made by Nomura Hanabi, a Mito company that has won Japan's top fireworks prize (the Prime Minister's Award) more times than anyone. World-class fireworks, in my hometown.
📍 Google Maps

🎇 Tsuchiura All-Japan Fireworks Competition

🚃🚌 Train + shuttle bus

Fireworks makers from all over Japan compete here — since 1925! About 20,000 fireworks in three categories: Star Mine, giant 10-go shells and creative fireworks. It's one of the most famous fireworks shows in Japan, and it happens in autumn, not summer.

When early November (2026: Sat, Nov 7) · Where Sakuragawa riverside, Tsuchiura
Paid shuttle bus from JR Tsuchiura Sta. (or about 30 min walk). VERY crowded — go early and dress warm!
📍 Google Maps

Dates and details change every year — please check the official sites before you go.

Souvenirs

Ibaraki Omiyage

What to bring home (or eat on the train)

In Japan, when you travel you bring omiyage (souvenirs) for family, friends and coworkers. Here are the Ibaraki classics locals actually buy.

🍠 Hoshiimo (dried sweet potato)Ibaraki makes most of Japan's hoshiimo. Chewy, sweet, no sugar added. Best in winter.
🍡 Mito no UmeMito's famous sweet: sweet bean paste wrapped in soft mochi and a shiso leaf soaked in plum syrup. Pretty and delicious.
🍘 Yoshiwara DenchuA traditional Mito snack — light puffed rice covered with roasted soybean flour (kinako). Old-school and gentle.
🫘 NattoOf course! Mito natto, sometimes wrapped in straw (wara natto). Keep it cold.
🍺 Hitachino Nest BeerThe craft beer with the little owl on the label, made by Kiuchi Brewery in Ibaraki. Popular all over the world.
🥢 Hitachi Aki SobaIbaraki is famous for soba! “Hitachi Aki Soba” is a buckwheat grown in northern Ibaraki — known for its rich smell. Buy dried soba to cook at home, or eat fresh soba at a local soba shop. My favorite one in Mito is in my Secret Restaurant List 🔒
🍶 Local sakeIbaraki has many old sake breweries. Ask the shop for a local one.
🏺 Kasama-yaki potteryA cup or a small plate from Kasama — a souvenir you can use every day.
🍈🌰 Melon & chestnut sweetsIbaraki is No.1 in Japan for melons and chestnuts, so look for sweets made with them.
Kaho's tip: where to buy
Mito Station has souvenir shops with most of these. For the best local stuff, stop at a michi-no-eki (roadside station) — cheap, fresh, and full of local snacks.
