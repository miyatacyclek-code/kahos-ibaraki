5 things that surprise people about rural Japan

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When people think of Japan, they think Tokyo: neon, crowded trains, tiny apartments. Rural Japan is a different world. I live in Ibaraki, in the countryside about one hour from Tokyo. Here's what surprises my followers the most.

1. You need a car

Trains and buses come maybe once an hour, sometimes less. Most families have one car per adult. Yes, really. Many people drive kei cars — small cars with yellow license plates. They are cheap, easy to park, and perfect for narrow farm roads.

2. Neighbors share vegetables

Someone grows too many cucumbers? You'll find a bag at your door. Then you give something back. It's a never-ending cycle 😂 You also see unmanned vegetable stands by the road: vegetables on a shelf and a small box for the money. It works on trust.

3. It's SO quiet at night

Except summer. Then the frogs in the rice fields are louder than Shibuya. You can also see so many more stars than in the city.

4. Garbage rules are serious

Burnable, plastic, cans, bottles, paper — each on a different day. Wrong day? Your bag stays there with a warning sticker. Everyone will know. Every town gives you a garbage calendar, and you really need it.

5. The convenience store is everything

Bank, post office, lunch, concert tickets, bills. In the countryside the konbini is the center of the town. Some even have big parking lots for trucks and tractors.

Bonus: houses are big (and cold)

Rent and land are much cheaper than in Tokyo, so houses are bigger, often with a garden or a small field. But many old houses have thin walls and no central heating, so winter is cold inside. That's why we love the kotatsu!

Slow, a little inconvenient… but I wouldn't trade it.

Want to see rural Japan?

You don't need to go far. From Tokyo, Ibaraki is easy for a day trip by train. You can see rice fields, the ocean, and small towns in one day. Rent a car if you can — it opens up so much more.
