Kashima Jingu: Ibaraki’s ancient shrine in the forest

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

If you want to feel “old Japan” near Tokyo, I think Kashima Jingu is one of the best places. It is in Kashima City, in the south-east of Ibaraki. People say it is the oldest and largest shrine in the Kanto region. The shrine’s tradition says it started in 660 BC, in the time of the first emperor, Jimmu.

What is Kashima Jingu?

The god here is Takemikazuchi, the god of martial arts. So many people who do kendo, judo or karate come to pray. The grounds are very big, about 70 hectares, with a forest of tall cedar trees and more than 600 kinds of plants.

What to see at Kashima Jingu

  • The Romon gate: a red, two-story gate about 13 meters tall, built in 1634. It is an Important Cultural Property and called one of Japan’s three great tower gates.
  • The cedar path: a quiet 300-meter path through the forest. Even on a busy day it feels calm.
  • The deer park: about 20 deer live here. Like in Nara, deer are seen as messengers of the god. The soccer team Kashima Antlers got its name from these deer!
  • Mitarashi Pond: a very clear pond fed by spring water. Long ago people cleaned their bodies here before praying.

The stone that stops earthquakes

Deep in the forest there is a small stone called Kaname-ishi. It looks tiny, but the legend says it is the top of a giant rock under the ground. In old stories, a huge catfish (namazu) under Japan makes earthquakes. The god of Kashima pushed it down and holds its head under this stone.

A tiny stone, a giant catfish, and a god holding Japan still.

How to get there from Tokyo

The easiest way is the Kashima Express highway bus from Tokyo Station (Yaesu South Exit). It takes around 2 hours to Kashimajingu Station. From there it is about a 10-minute walk. By train, you can go to Mito and take the Oarai-Kashima Line (about 1 hour 15 minutes). IC cards do not work on that line, so buy a ticket.

Is it free?

Yes! Entering the shrine grounds is free, and you can visit any time. Late autumn is a lovely time to walk in the cool forest.
