Hitachi-gyu: Ibaraki’s own wagyu beef

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When people hear “wagyu,” they often think of Kobe or Matsusaka. But Ibaraki has its own famous wagyu too. It is called Hitachi-gyu (常陸牛). In English it is also called Hitachi Wagyu or Hitachi Beef.

What is Hitachi-gyu?

Not every cow from Ibaraki can be called Hitachi-gyu. The rules are strict:

  • It must be Japanese Black cattle (kuroge wagyu).
  • It is raised for about 30 months by designated farmers in Ibaraki.
  • The meat must get a yield grade of A or B, and a quality grade of 4 or 5. So only beef graded A4, A5, B4 or B5 can use the name.

Only the top grades can wear the Hitachi-gyu name.

A little history

Ibaraki’s cattle story is old. The official Hitachi-gyu site says it goes back to a farm that Tokugawa Nariaki, the lord of Mito, made in 1832. The name “Hitachi-gyu” was born in 1976, and the promotion association started in 1977. Hitachi is also the old name of this area.

How to eat it

The soft, marbled parts like sirloin, loin and fillet are great for steak. Thin slices are perfect for yakiniku, sukiyaki and shabu-shabu. Now is a great season for a hot sukiyaki or shabu-shabu pot!

How do you know it is real?

Each Hitachi-gyu gets a certificate with a 10-digit ID number. With this number, you can check where the cow came from on Japan’s beef tracking system. Shops and restaurants that sell it are registered with the Hitachi-gyu association.

Where can you try it?

  • Look for registered restaurants and butcher shops in Ibaraki. The prefecture’s food site, Ibaraki Shokusai (ibaraki-shokusai.net), has lists of shops where you can buy it and eat it.
  • Hitachi-gyu is also sold to some high-end Japanese restaurants abroad. The official site lists places in Bangkok, Vietnam and Singapore.
  • Wagyu is a treat, so check the price on the menu before you order.
