Ofuro: why Japanese families share the same bathwater

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Here is a fact that surprises many people outside Japan: in a lot of Japanese homes, the whole family uses the same bathwater in one evening. One person gets out, the next person gets in. Same tub, same hot water.

It sounds strange at first. But in Japan, it is just normal life, especially on cold winter nights.

The bath is not for washing

The key is this: in Japan, you wash your body before you get in the bath. A Japanese bathroom has a shower and a washing area next to the tub. You sit on a small stool, wash your body and hair, and rinse off all the soap. Only then do you get into the tub.

So the tub is only for soaking and warming up. When everyone goes in clean, the water stays clean too. That is why sharing it is not a problem.

In Japan, the bathtub is not for getting clean. It is for getting warm.

Who goes first?

In the old days, the order was about respect. A guest was offered the first bath. Then the father, and then the rest of the family. Today many families are more relaxed, and the order often just depends on who comes home first.

Japanese people usually take a bath at night, not in the morning. The water is often hot, around 40 to 42°C. Many bathtubs have a lid to keep the heat in, and modern ones have a button called oidaki (追い焚き) that reheats the water for the next person.

Nothing is wasted

Some families use the leftover bathwater again the next day for laundry. Some Japanese washing machines even have a small pump that takes water from the bathtub. It saves water, and it is a very Japanese idea: use things until the end.

Tips for visitors

  • At a ryokan, onsen or a Japanese home, always wash and rinse before you get in.
  • Never pull the plug! Other people may still be waiting to use the water.
  • Do not put your towel in the water. Keep it on your head or by the side.
  • Put the lid back on when you finish, so the water stays warm.

Want to try a Japanese bath in Ibaraki? Fukuroda Onsen, near Fukuroda Falls in Daigo, has hot spring inns where you can take a day-trip bath. Check hours before you go.
