Carb on carb: why Japan eats ramen with rice

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Recently a funny argument went around social media. Someone outside Japan joked that a Japanese meal of ramen, gyoza and a rice dish was “a crime.” Three kinds of carbs on one tray! Japanese people answered with jokes too: “Why not add fried rice?”

For many foreigners this is a real surprise. But for us, carb on carb is just normal lunch.

What does carb on carb look like?

  • Ramen + fried rice + gyoza. Many ramen shops and small Chinese-style restaurants sell this as a “set.” Often the fried rice is a half size.
  • Okonomiyaki + rice. In Osaka, many okonomiyaki shops have a lunch set with rice and miso soup.
  • Yakisoba-pan. Fried noodles inside a soft bread roll. You can find it at bakeries and convenience stores.
  • Soba or udon + a small rice bowl. A noodle shop lunch set often comes with a mini katsudon or a rice ball.

In Japan, noodles are often the side dish. Rice is the meal.

Why do Japanese people eat like this?

For a long time, Japanese meals were built around rice. A normal meal is rice, soup and some small dishes to eat with the rice. So for many people, a meal without rice feels like it is not finished.

Ramen, gyoza and fried rice also came from Chinese cooking, but Japan changed them a lot. Even the famous tenshinhan (crab omelet on rice) is a Japanese creation. Together they became a classic “machi-chuka” lunch: cheap, hot and very filling.

And for workers, a set meal is easy. One order, one price, and you are full until dinner.

Tips for visitors

  • Look for the word セット (setto) or 定食 (teishoku) on the menu. It is usually cheaper than ordering each dish.
  • If you are not so hungry, choose ハーフ (hāfu), a half size, or just order the noodles alone. Nobody will think it is strange.
  • Small local diners in the countryside are a great place to try this. They are cheap, and you eat what local people really eat.

So, is it a crime?

Maybe for your diet. But on a cold winter day in Japan, a hot bowl of ramen with a little fried rice is pure happiness.
