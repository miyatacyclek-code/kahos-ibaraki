Fukuroda Falls in autumn: a giant waterfall and red leaves

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When people ask me for one place in Ibaraki they can't miss, I often say: Fukuroda Falls (袋田の滝). It is in Daigo town, in the mountains of north Ibaraki. And right now, in November, the mountains around the falls turn red, orange and yellow.

The photo at the top is mine. I took it from the first viewing deck, right in front of the falls. Photos can't show how loud and powerful it is!

White water, red leaves, and the sound of the falls in your whole body.

What is Fukuroda Falls?

  • It is about 120 meters high and 73 meters wide.
  • The water falls down a big rock wall in four steps, so it is also called “Yodo no Taki” (the four-step falls).
  • It is one of the three great waterfalls of Japan, with Kegon Falls in Nikko and Nachi Falls in Wakayama.
  • It is a national Place of Scenic Beauty.

There is an old story about the name, too. A famous poet monk, Saigyo, is said to have visited and said you must come once in each of the four seasons to really enjoy this waterfall. I agree with him!

When to see the autumn leaves

The leaves start to change color in late October. The best time is usually early to mid November — around November 10 in a normal year. The trees around the falls are maples and other mountain trees, so you get a mix of red, orange and yellow.

The color changes with the weather every year, so check the latest leaf status before you go. On weekends in the best season, it gets very busy. If you can, go on a weekday or early in the morning.

The other seasons

  • Spring: fresh green leaves.
  • Summer: deep green and cool air. A nice escape from the heat.
  • Winter: in very cold years, the falls can freeze! This is called “hyōbaku” (ice falls). It does not happen every year, so it feels really special.

How to see the falls: the tunnel and viewing decks

You walk to the falls through a long tunnel (about 276 meters). At the end is the first viewing deck, right in front of the falls. You can feel the water spray.

Then take the elevator up to the second viewing deck, about 50 meters higher. From there you can see all four steps of the falls at once. For autumn leaf photos, this is the best spot.

Outside the tunnel there is also a suspension bridge over the river, and you can see the falls from there too.

Tickets and opening hours

  • Tunnel and viewing decks: 500 yen for adults, 300 yen for children (elementary and junior high school).
  • May to October: 8:00–18:00. November: 8:00–17:00. December to April: 9:00–17:00.
  • Open every day.
  • In the 2025 season there was an evening light-up from November to January. Check this year's dates before you go.

Prices and times can change, so please check the official information before your trip.

How to get there from Tokyo

By train: Take the JR Joban Line limited express from Tokyo or Ueno to Mito Station. At Mito, change to the JR Suigun Line and go to Fukuroda Station. From Fukuroda Station, take the bus to “Takimoto” (about 10 minutes) and walk about 10 minutes. You can also walk from the station, but it takes about 40 minutes.

The Suigun Line is a slow, pretty local train along the Kuji River. I love it. But it does not come often, so check the train times before you go, and also the times for your way back.

By car: From Naka IC on the Joban Expressway, it takes about 50 to 60 minutes. There are free town parking lots and also paid private parking near the falls. From the free parking lot it is a walk of about 1 km. In the leaf season, the roads near the falls get busy, so leave early.

Tips if you go

  • Go early in the morning, especially on weekends in November.
  • Wear warm clothes. The mountains are colder than Tokyo, and the air near the falls is cool.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. There are some slopes and stairs around the falls.
  • Bring some cash for small shops and food stalls.
  • Daigo is also known for apples. In autumn you can find apple orchards and apple sweets in the area.
  • Want more nature? There are hiking trails near the falls, too.

More Ibaraki in autumn

November is a delicious month in Ibaraki. Try the new Hitachi Aki Soba noodles, or go to the sea for Nakaminato Fish Market. And if you want flowers next autumn, don't miss the red kochia at Hitachi Seaside Park.
