Ibaraki Museum of History: Mito’s golden ginkgo avenue

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When people think of autumn in Japan, they think of red maple leaves. But in Mito, the capital of Ibaraki, there is a famous yellow spot too. It is the ginkgo avenue at the Ibaraki Prefectural Museum of History (茨城県立歴史館).

What is this place?

The museum opened in 1974. It has a big garden, about 72,000 square meters. In the garden you can see old buildings, like a house from the Edo period and the main building of the old Mitsukaido Elementary School, a Western-style school from the Meiji period. In autumn, the ginkgo trees turn bright yellow, and the path looks like gold.

A golden tunnel of leaves, and the garden is free.

The ginkgo festival and light-up

Every autumn the museum holds the “Rekishikan Icho Matsuri” (ginkgo festival). In 2026, it runs from November 1 to 23. During the festival, the garden is lit up every evening from 17:00 to 20:00, even on days the museum is closed. Entry to the garden is free.

There are also small events, like a market and a street piano under the ginkgo trees. They change every year, so check the museum website before you go.

Is the museum free?

  • Garden: free
  • Museum inside: ¥180 for adults, ¥90 for people 70 and over
  • High school students and younger: free
  • Open 9:30–17:00 (last entry 16:30)
  • Closed on Mondays (if Monday is a holiday, it closes on Tuesday instead)

How to get there

From JR Mito Station North Exit, take a bus from stop No. 4 bound for Sakuragawa Nishi-danchi. Get off at “Rekishikan Kairakuen Iriguchi”. The ride is about 10 minutes, then about 2 minutes on foot. A taxi from the station is also about 10 minutes. By car, it is about 15 minutes from Mito IC on the Joban Expressway. The parking lot is small in the festival season, so a bus is easier.

Tips

  • Kairakuen, Mito’s famous garden, is very close. You can see both in one day.
  • Go in the evening for the light-up. The yellow leaves look magical at night.
  • Bring a warm jacket. November nights in Ibaraki are cold!
