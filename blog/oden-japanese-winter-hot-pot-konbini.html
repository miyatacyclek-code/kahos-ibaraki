Oden: Japan’s winter hot pot you can buy at the konbini

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When the nights get cold in Japan, a little steaming pot appears next to the register at many convenience stores. Inside, things like radish, eggs and fish cakes are slowly simmering in a light brown soup. This is oden (おでん), one of Japan’s favorite winter comfort foods.

What is oden?

Oden is a kind of hot pot. Many ingredients are simmered for a long time in dashi, a Japanese soup stock made from things like kelp and dried bonito. Nothing is fried at the end and nothing is spicy. The taste is gentle, and the ingredients soak up the soup.

  • Daikon radish: soft, juicy, and full of soup. Many people say it is the best one
  • Boiled egg: brown on the outside after hours in the pot
  • Konnyaku: a jelly-like block with almost no calories
  • Fish cakes: chikuwa (a tube), hanpen (soft and fluffy) and many more
  • Atsuage: thick fried tofu

Many people add a little karashi, a strong Japanese mustard. Just a small dot is enough.

Where did it come from?

Oden grew out of an old dish called dengaku: tofu on a stick with miso. Over time, people started to simmer the ingredients in soup instead, and the short name “oden” stayed. In 1979, 7-Eleven Japan started selling oden in its stores, and today the konbini pot is a winter sign for many Japanese people.

In Japan, winter smells like dashi at the konbini.

Every region has its own oden

Oden changes from place to place. In the Kansai area, the soup is usually lighter in color. Shizuoka is famous for its very dark soup made with beef tendon, with fish powder and green seaweed sprinkled on top. In the countryside, like here in Ibaraki, a warm konbini on a dark, cold road can feel like a small lighthouse.

How to order oden at a konbini

  • Look for the pot near the register. Not every store has one, and some sell oden in ready-made cups
  • Tell the staff what you want, or just point. They put it in a cup with soup for you
  • Ask for “karashi” if you want mustard
  • Start with daikon, egg and one fish cake. It is a cheap, warm snack on a cold day
