Kasama Chrysanthemum Festival: Ibaraki’s flower shrine

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

November in Japan is chrysanthemum season. And in Ibaraki, the most famous place to see them is Kasama. Every autumn, Kasama Inari Shrine and the streets around it fill with chrysanthemums for the Kasama Chrysanthemum Festival (笠間の菊まつり). It is called the oldest chrysanthemum festival in Japan, and 2026 is already the 119th time!

When is the Kasama Chrysanthemum Festival 2026?

This year it runs from October 24 to November 23, 2026. Entry to the shrine is free. Some special areas cost money.

What can you see?

  • About 10,000 pots of chrysanthemums around the shrine and the town
  • Kiku ningyo: life-size dolls dressed in real chrysanthemum flowers
  • Big “cascade” chrysanthemums and “thousand-bloom” plants with hundreds of flowers on one plant
  • Flower-decorated water basins for washing your hands, great for photos

A whole shrine dressed in flowers, only for one month a year.

About Kasama Inari Shrine

Kasama Inari Shrine is known as one of the three biggest Inari shrines in Japan. Legend says it was founded in 651. Inari is the god of rice and good business, so you will see many fox statues. On the street in front of the shrine, try inari sushi. Some shops put walnuts inside, because the shrine’s old name is Kurumishita Inari (“under the walnut tree”).

Other events this year

  • Yabusame (horseback archery): November 3
  • Yappe Matsuri night festival on the shrine street: November 22, 6–9 PM
  • Special festival goshuin (shrine stamp) at the shrine

How to get to Kasama Inari Shrine

By train, go to JR Kasama Station on the Mito Line. From there it is about a 20-minute walk, or a short bus ride. From Ueno, one way is a limited express to Tomobe, then change to the Mito Line for Kasama. By car, it is about 15 minutes from Tomobe IC on the Kitakanto Expressway.

Tips for visiting

  • Weekends are busy. Come in the morning, or on a weekday, for quieter photos.
  • The flowers look best from early to mid November, when most of them are fully open.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. You will walk around the shrine and the shopping street.
  • Some small shops only take cash.

Kasama Chrysanthemum Festival FAQ

  • Is it free? Yes, entry to the shrine grounds is free. Some special exhibits cost a small fee.
  • How long do I need? About 1 to 2 hours for the shrine and the flowers. Half a day if you add the pottery town.
  • Can I visit from Tokyo in one day? Yes. It's an easy day trip by train or car.

Kasama is also a pottery town, so you can look at Kasama-yaki after the flowers. If you go, share your chrysanthemum photos with #KahoIbaraki, or tell me in the YouTube comments!
