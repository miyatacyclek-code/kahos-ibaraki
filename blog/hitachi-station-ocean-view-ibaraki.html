Hitachi Station: the glass train station over the ocean

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Most train stations in Japan are busy, grey and practical. You walk in, you tap your card, you leave. But in Hitachi City, on the coast of Ibaraki, there is a station people visit just to stand still and look.

Walk along the passage at Hitachi Station and the wall turns into glass. In front of you there is nothing but sky and the Pacific Ocean. It almost feels like the station is floating above the sea.

Who designed it?

The station building was finished in 2011. Its design was led by Kazuyo Sejima, an architect who grew up in Hitachi. Together with her partner Ryue Nishizawa (their office is called SANAA), she won the Pritzker Prize in 2010. People often call it the Nobel Prize of architecture.

In 2014 the station also won a prize at the Brunel Awards, an international design competition just for railway buildings. Not bad for a small city station!

A station you visit not to leave, but to stay a little longer.

Why stations matter so much in Japan

In Japan, especially outside Tokyo, the station is often the center of the town. Shops, bus stops and meeting points are all around it. Many local cities rebuild their station to give the town a new face, and Hitachi chose the ocean. It is a quiet way of saying: this is who we are.

SEA BiRDS CAFE

At the end of the glass passage there is a small glass-box cafe called SEA BiRDS CAFE. You can sit with a drink and watch the waves while the trains come and go behind you. Because the station faces east, some people come early in the morning for the sunrise.

Tips for visiting

  • The glass passage is a public walkway, so you can see the view without a train ticket.
  • Hitachi Station is on the JR Joban Line. Limited express trains from Ueno or Tokyo take you there in about 1.5 to 2 hours. Check the timetable before you go.
  • Go on a clear day. The blue of the sea is the whole show.
  • Combine it with Hitachi Seaside Park or Oiwa Shrine for a full day in northern Ibaraki.
