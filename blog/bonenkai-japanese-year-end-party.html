Bonenkai: Japan’s “forget the year” party

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Walk past an izakaya on a Friday night in December, and you will often hear a loud “Kanpai!” from a big table inside. Many of those groups are at a bonenkai (忘年会).

What is a bonenkai?

Bonenkai means “forget-the-year party.” 忘 means forget, 年 means year, and 会 means gathering. The idea is simple: forget the troubles of this year and start the new year fresh.

Coworkers, sports teams, old school friends and even neighbors hold them. Most happen in December. In January, some groups have another one called shinnenkai (新年会), a “new year party” that looks forward instead of back.

How a bonenkai works

Most bonenkai are at an izakaya. The organizer books a set course meal with nomihodai (飲み放題), all-you-can-drink for a fixed time, often two hours. Everyone pays the same amount. When the party ends, many people go on to a nijikai (二次会), a second party at a bar or karaoke.

Nobody drinks before the kanpai. Nobody pours their own glass.

The unwritten drinking rules

Japanese drinking parties have small rules that nobody writes down:

  • Wait until everyone has a drink. Then someone gives a short speech, and everyone says “Kanpai!” together. Do not sip before that.
  • Do not pour your own drink. Pour for the people next to you, and they will pour for you.
  • When someone pours for you, hold your glass up with your hand.
  • Watch the glasses around you. If one is almost empty, offer to fill it.

These rules come from a culture of caring about the group. At work, it is also a way to say thank you to your boss and coworkers for the year. With close friends, people are much more relaxed.

Is the bonenkai changing?

Yes. Many younger workers say they do not like long drinking nights with the boss, and the number of big office parties went down during the pandemic years. But recent surveys in Japan found that many people in their 20s and 30s still feel positive about year-end parties, as long as they are short and fun. Today you also see lunch parties and parties with no pressure to drink.

Tips for visitors

  • In December, izakaya in cities and even in small towns are very busy, especially on Friday nights. Book ahead or go early.
  • If a Japanese friend invites you, you do not have to drink alcohol. Oolong tea or a soft drink is fine. Just join the kanpai.
  • Try pouring for the person next to you. It is a small act, but people will love it.
