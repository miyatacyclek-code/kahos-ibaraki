I went to see the kochia!

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

I finally went to see the kochia at Hitachi Seaside Park! I went early in the morning — and people were already waiting in line! Kochia is that popular. In spring this park is famous for the blue nemophila flowers. But in autumn, the hill changes completely.

Kochia are round, fluffy plants. In summer they are bright green. Then in autumn they slowly turn red, like the whole hill is blushing.

It looks like someone put thousands of red pom-poms on a hill.

Fun facts about kochia

  • In Japanese it's also called “hōkigi” — broom tree. People used to dry it and make brooms!
  • Its seeds are eaten in Akita as “tonburi”, also called “field caviar”.
  • The color changes week by week: green → pink-red → red → golden brown.

Tips if you go

  • Best red color is usually in October — check the park's website for the latest status
  • Go early in the morning — but even early, there was already a line when I got there! Arrive before opening if you can
  • Walk up to the top of the hill. You can see the ocean too!

Spring blue, autumn red. Same hill, two totally different worlds. This is why I love Ibaraki.

Why I keep sharing Ibaraki

Ibaraki is often ranked Japan's “least attractive” prefecture. Honestly, that makes me a little sad — and a little angry 😤

Because look at this place! Beautiful nature like this, only about one hour from Tokyo. Most people just don't know about it yet. Even many Japanese people.

I want to change Ibaraki's image — and show the whole world how beautiful it is.

So I make videos, I write this blog, and I made this app. If even one person visits Ibaraki because of me, I'll be so happy.
