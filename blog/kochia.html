I went to see the kochia!

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

I finally went to see the kochia at Hitachi Seaside Park! I went early in the morning — and people were already waiting in line! Kochia is that popular. In spring this park is famous for the blue nemophila flowers. But in autumn, the hill changes completely.

Kochia are round, fluffy plants. In summer they are bright green. Then in autumn they slowly turn red, like the whole hill is blushing.

It looks like someone put thousands of red pom-poms on a hill.

In this post I share everything you need for your visit: the best time, tickets, how to get there from Tokyo, and my tips as a local.

Fun facts about kochia

  • In Japanese it's also called “hōkigi” — broom tree. People used to dry it and make brooms!
  • Its seeds are eaten in Akita as “tonburi”, also called “field caviar”.
  • The color changes week by week: green → pink-red → red → golden brown.

When is the best time to see the kochia?

The kochia grow on Miharashi Hill (みはらしの丘), the same hill where the nemophila bloom in spring. In summer, from July to August, they are fresh green. They start to turn red in late September. The best red is usually in October.

After that, the kochia slowly turn golden brown. Honestly, golden kochia are pretty too, and there are fewer people.

In 2026, the park held its “Kochia Carnival” from September 18 to November 3. The dates and the color change a little every year, so check the official Hitachi Seaside Park website before you go. They show the latest color status there.

Tickets and opening hours

  • Adults (high school age and up): 450 yen. Seniors (65+): 210 yen. Junior high school students and younger: free.
  • In the peak flower seasons (April, May and October) there is an extra seasonal price. In 2026 it was 800 yen for adults in the kochia season (October 9 to November 3).
  • Open from 9:30 to 17:00. From November to February the park closes at 16:30.
  • The park is usually closed on Tuesdays, but in the busy flower seasons it is often open every day. Check the calendar on the official website.
  • Car parking is 600 yen for one day.

I recommend bringing some cash, just in case.

How to get there from Tokyo

By train and bus: Take the JR Joban Line limited express (Hitachi or Tokiwa) from Tokyo, Shinagawa or Ueno to Katsuta Station. It takes about 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes. At Katsuta Station, go to the East Exit and take the bus from stop No. 2 to “Kaihin Koen Nishiguchi” (Seaside Park West Gate). The bus takes about 15 minutes. So from Tokyo it is about 2 hours in total.

By car: The park is right next to Hitachi Kaihin Koen IC. On weekends in October the roads and parking lots near the park get very busy, so leave early.

Buses from Katsuta are not every few minutes like in Tokyo. Check the bus time for your way back, too!

Tips if you go

  • Go early in the morning — but even early, there was already a line when I got there! Arrive before opening if you can.
  • Walk up to the top of the hill. You can see the ocean too!
  • The park is really big. You can rent a bicycle (600 yen for 3 hours for adults) and ride on the cycling paths.
  • Tired legs or small kids? The Seaside Train goes around the park. A one-day ticket is 600 yen.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. The path up the hill is a small climb.
  • Bring a jacket. The wind from the ocean can be cold in autumn.
  • Please stay on the path and don't step into the kochia field.

Make it a day trip

The park is in Hitachinaka, near the sea. For lunch, go to Nakaminato Fish Market for fresh seafood bowls. In the afternoon you can see the torii gate on the rocks in Oarai. I made a full plan in my post Easy one-day trip to Ibaraki from Tokyo.

Spring blue, autumn red. Same hill, two totally different worlds. This is why I love Ibaraki.

Why I keep sharing Ibaraki

Ibaraki is often ranked Japan's “least attractive” prefecture. Honestly, that makes me a little sad — and a little angry 😤

Because look at this place! Beautiful nature like this, only about one hour from Tokyo. Most people just don't know about it yet. Even many Japanese people.

I want to change Ibaraki's image — and show the whole world how beautiful it is.

So I make videos, I write this blog, and I made this app. If even one person visits Ibaraki because of me, I'll be so happy.
