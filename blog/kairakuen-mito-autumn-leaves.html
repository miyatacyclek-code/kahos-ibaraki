Kairakuen in autumn: Mito’s maple valley in Ibaraki

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When people hear “Kairakuen,” they think of plum flowers in February. That is fair! But this big garden in Mito, Ibaraki is quiet and beautiful in autumn too, and I think more people should know.

What is Kairakuen?

Kairakuen is one of the “Three Great Gardens of Japan,” together with Kenrokuen in Kanazawa and Korakuen in Okayama. It was made in 1842 by Tokugawa Nariaki, the 9th lord of the Mito Domain. It has about 3,000 plum trees of around 100 kinds.

Where are the autumn leaves?

Look for Momijidani, the “maple valley.” It has about 170 maple trees that turn red and orange. In past years there was a free evening light-up there in November, so check the official site for this year’s dates.

  • Momijidani: red maple leaves
  • A bamboo grove and cedar woods: cool and green, very calm
  • Kobuntei: an old Japanese-style building with a view of the garden and Senba Lake (extra fee)
  • Tokiwa Shrine: right next to the garden

Everyone comes for the plums. Come in autumn, and the garden is almost yours.

How to get to Kairakuen

From Mito Station, take a bus from the north exit to the “Kairakuen” stop. It is about 15 minutes. Or you can walk about 20–30 minutes along Senba Lake. I really like the walk on a sunny day. A special temporary station (Kairakuen Station) only opens during the plum festival, so in autumn please use Mito Station.

Tips for your visit

  • The garden opens early in the morning, so you can visit before the crowds
  • Wear comfortable shoes, there are slopes and stone steps
  • Bring a warm jacket, late autumn evenings get cold
  • Check the official Kairakuen website for hours and fees before you go
