No tipping in Japan: why the staff may run after you with your change

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Imagine this. You finish a nice dinner in Japan, you leave some coins on the table, and you walk out. A few seconds later, the waiter runs after you down the street. “Excuse me! You forgot your money!”

This really happens, and many visitors love telling the story. In Japan, there is no tipping culture. Not in cities, and not in the countryside either.

Why don’t people tip in Japan?

In Japan, good service is simply part of the job. Staff are paid to do their work well, and they don’t expect anything extra for it. The price on the menu is the price.

Also, since April 2021, shops and restaurants must show prices with the consumption tax already included. So in most places, what you see is what you pay. No extra tax at the register, no tip line on the receipt.

Because of this, extra money can make people feel confused, or a little uncomfortable. Some staff will politely refuse it. Others will think you made a mistake, and chase you to give it back.

In Japan, the best tip is a simple “gochisousama deshita.”

Are there any exceptions?

  • Some higher-end hotels, ryokan and restaurants add a service charge, often around 10%. It is already on the bill, so you don’t need to add anything.
  • At some traditional ryokan, a few guests give a small thank-you gift of money called kokorozuke (心付け). It is optional, and it is always put in a small envelope, never handed over as loose cash. Many ryokan don’t expect it at all.
  • If you are unsure, it is totally fine to just not tip. Nobody will think you are rude.

How to say thank you instead

Words matter more than money here. When you leave a restaurant, say “gochisousama deshita” (ごちそうさまでした). It means something like “thank you for the meal,” and in small family restaurants the owner will usually smile and answer you.

In shops, a small bow and “arigatou gozaimasu” is perfect. In the countryside, where many places are run by the same family for years, coming back again is the biggest compliment of all.

A tip for visitors

Pay at the register, take your change, and say thank you with a smile. That’s it. It is one of the easiest parts of traveling in Japan.
