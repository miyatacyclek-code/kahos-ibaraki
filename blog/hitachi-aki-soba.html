Hitachi Aki Soba: Ibaraki’s autumn noodles

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Do you like soba? Soba is Japanese noodles made from buckwheat. In Ibaraki, November is a special time for soba lovers. Why? Because the new Hitachi Aki Soba is here!

What is Hitachi Aki Soba?

“Aki” means autumn. Hitachi Aki Soba is a buckwheat variety from Hitachi-Ota City, in the north of Ibaraki. People made it better by choosing the best local plants again and again. In 1985, Ibaraki Prefecture chose it as a variety to promote. Hitachi-Ota has been famous for soba since the Edo period. The days are warm, the nights are cold, and the fields are on slopes with good soil. Buckwheat loves that!

From flower to noodle

  • Farmers sow the seeds in mid-August.
  • Pretty white flowers bloom in September.
  • The harvest is in late October.
  • The seeds are dried for about one week, then milled into flour.

So the new soba is ready around November. The seeds are big and even. The noodles have a rich smell and a soft, sweet taste.

Autumn in Ibaraki tastes like fresh soba.

How to eat it

  • Zaru soba: cold noodles on a bamboo tray. The simple way, best to taste the soba itself.
  • Kenchin soba: soba in a warm soup with daikon, carrot, burdock root, konjac and mushrooms.
  • Kenchin tsuke soba: dip cold soba into the warm kenchin soup. Perfect on a chilly day!

Some places in Ibaraki hold soba festivals in this season, and you can also try a soba-making class. Popular soba shops can sell out early, so go early in the day if you visit!
