Easy one-day trip to Ibaraki from Tokyo

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Many travelers ask me: “Can I visit Ibaraki without a car?” Yes! Here's an easy route. Flowers by the sea, a seafood lunch, and a torii gate standing on the rocks in the ocean. All in one day from Tokyo.

The plan in short

  • Morning: Hitachi Seaside Park
  • Lunch: Nakaminato Fish Market
  • Afternoon: Oarai Isosaki Shrine
  • Evening: back to Tokyo from Mito

Getting to Ibaraki

Take the JR Joban Line limited express (Hitachi or Tokiwa) from Tokyo, Shinagawa or Ueno to Katsuta Station. It takes about 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes. If you have a Japan Rail Pass, it covers the JR trains.

Start early! A train around 7 or 8 am is perfect.

Morning: Hitachi Seaside Park

From Katsuta Station East Exit, take the bus from stop No. 2 to “Kaihin Koen Nishiguchi” (Seaside Park West Gate). It takes about 15 minutes. Go early — especially in spring!

The park opens at 9:30. Adults pay 450 yen, with an extra seasonal price in April, May and October. The best seasons are the blue nemophila (mid-April to early May) and the red kochia (October). The park is huge, so plan 2 to 3 hours. Renting a bicycle is a good idea.

Lunch: Nakaminato Fish Market

Fresh sushi and seafood bowls. Take the bus back to Katsuta Station, then the small Minato Line train about 15 minutes to Nakaminato Station, and walk 10 to 15 minutes. If you are a group, a taxi from the park is faster. Come hungry. More info in my Nakaminato Fish Market guide.

Tip: on weekends in the nemophila season, there is a free shuttle bus between the park and Ajigaura Station on the Minato Line. Then you can go straight to Nakaminato by train.

Afternoon: Oarai Isosaki Shrine

Oarai is just across the river from Nakaminato. A taxi is the easiest way. By train, go to Oarai Station and take the “Kaiyu-go” loop bus to “Oarai Isosaki Jinja Shita” (about 15 minutes).

This shrine is very old. It is said to date from the year 856. The famous view is the “Kamiiso no Torii”, a torii gate standing on the rocks in the sea, with the waves hitting it. You can't walk to the gate, but you can see it close from the beach path. The shrine is free to visit.

The torii faces east, so sunrise is the most famous view. Many people come on New Year's morning. But the afternoon is nice and calm too.

Waves, rocks and a torii gate. It feels like a place where gods really came down.

Back to Tokyo

From Oarai Station, take the Kashima Rinkai Railway to Mito (about 15 minutes). From Mito, the JR Joban Line limited express goes back to Tokyo in about 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes.

Tips

  • Check bus times before you go — they are not frequent
  • Bring cash for small shops. Small local trains and buses may not take IC cards like Suica.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. You will walk a lot.
  • In winter, try ankō (monkfish) hot pot, a famous Ibaraki winter food.
  • Buy hoshiimo (dried sweet potato) as a souvenir (omiyage)

Then you can say: I've been to the real Japan!
