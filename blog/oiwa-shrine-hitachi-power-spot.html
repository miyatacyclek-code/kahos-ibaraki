Oiwa Shrine: I went to Ibaraki’s most famous power spot

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

This weekend I went to Oiwa Shrine (御岩神社) in Hitachi with my friend and her little daughter. It is in the mountains of north Ibaraki, and many Japanese people call it one of the strongest power spots in the country.

We went early in the morning. The parking lot already had many cars from outside Ibaraki. I was a little surprised. So it is true: this small shrine in my prefecture is famous all over Japan.

What is a “power spot”?

In Japan, a power spot (パワースポット) is a place where people feel special energy from nature or the gods. Usually it is an old shrine, a big tree, a waterfall or a mountain. People go there to feel calm, to pray, or just to get some good energy for their life.

Why Oiwa Shrine is special

Nobody knows exactly how old it is. People found remains from the Jomon period here, thousands of years ago, and the shrine already appears in the Hitachi no Kuni Fudoki, a very old record of this area. Back then the mountain was called “Kabire no Takamine.”

Many gods are worshipped here, about 188, which is very rare. For a long time Shinto and Buddhism were mixed here too, so you can feel both as you walk.

Don’t miss the Sanbon-sugi, the Three Cedars. It is one giant old cedar tree that splits into three trunks. Standing under it, I felt very small, in a good way.

There is also a famous story: people say an astronaut saw a light coming from this area from space. True or not? I don’t know. But it makes the walk more fun.

The air was different

Early morning, cold air, giant cedars. I could not stop taking deep breaths.

The path goes through a forest of tall cedar trees. It was cold and very quiet. Even with many visitors, everyone was talking softly. The air felt clean and… sacred is the only word I have.

We did not talk much. We just walked and breathed. When we left, I felt lighter. I hope some good things happen to us now 🙏

I filmed this trip too, and I will upload the video to my YouTube soon. Please look forward to it!

Tips for visitors

1. Go early. In the morning it is quiet and the air is the best. Weekends get busy later in the day.

2. Wear good shoes. The main shrine area is easy, but if you go up to the top of Mt. Oiwa (530 m), it is a real mountain trail.

3. Shrine manners. Bow once at the torii gate, walk on the side of the path (the center is for the gods), and wash your hands at the water basin before you pray.

4. Be respectful. It is a working shrine, not a theme park. Keep your voice low and check if photos are OK in each area.

How to get there

By train and bus: take the JR Joban Line to Hitachi Station. From bus stop No. 1, take bus No. 60 (for Higashigodo) and get off at Oiwa Jinja. A taxi from the station is also easy.

By car: about 10 minutes from Hitachi-Chuo IC on the Joban Expressway, along Route 36. Car is easiest, especially if you want to visit other places in north Ibaraki on the same day.

Oiwa Shrine is a perfect start for a north Ibaraki nature day. After the shrine, you can go to Fukuroda Falls or eat Hitachi Aki Soba. Find the plan in my guide app, and if you go, post with #KahoIbaraki. I want to see!
