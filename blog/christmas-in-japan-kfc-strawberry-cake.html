Christmas in Japan: fried chicken, strawberry cake and no day off

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

If you visit Japan in December, you will see Christmas lights everywhere. But Christmas Day here is a normal work and school day. And the most famous Christmas dinner is not turkey. It is fried chicken from KFC.

Christmas is not a holiday

December 25 is not a public holiday in Japan. Only a small number of people are Christian, so for most families Christmas is not a religious day. It is a fun, cute season with lights, music and special food. The big family holiday comes one week later: New Year, when people go back to their hometown and visit a shrine.

Why KFC?

In 1974, KFC Japan started a campaign called “Kentucky for Christmas”. The story KFC tells is that a foreign customer said he could not find turkey in Japan, so fried chicken became the next best thing. The timing was perfect. Japanese people were just starting to enjoy Western-style Christmas food, and the idea stuck.

Today many families order a Christmas barrel weeks before. Reservations usually open in November, and people pick up their chicken around December 19 to 25. If you just walk in on Christmas Eve, be ready for a long line.

In Japan, Christmas dinner is a bucket of fried chicken, and nobody thinks it is strange.

The white strawberry cake

The other must-have is Christmas cake. In Japan this means a soft sponge cake with whipped cream and fresh strawberries on top. The confectionery company Fujiya made its strawberry shortcake in 1922 and helped make it a Christmas dessert in the 1950s. Now bakeries, supermarkets and even convenience stores take cake orders in November.

Christmas Eve is the big night

In Japan, December 24 is more important than December 25. Families eat chicken and cake at home. For young couples, Christmas Eve is a romantic date night, a bit like Valentine’s Day in other countries. Nice restaurants and illumination spots get very busy.

Tips for visitors

  • Want KFC on Christmas Eve? Reserve early, or go on a different day.
  • Try a slice of Christmas cake from a konbini. It is cheap and really good.
  • Don’t expect shops to close on December 25. Most are open as usual.
  • But many places close around New Year (about December 29 to January 3), so check before you travel.
