Hoshiimo season is here!

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When the wind gets cold, Ibaraki starts making hoshiimo (干し芋) — dried sweet potato. If you visit Ibaraki in winter, you will see it everywhere: in supermarkets, at roadside stations, and in gift shops at the train station.

Ibaraki makes about 90% of all the hoshiimo in Japan. Most of it comes from Hitachinaka, Tokai and Naka, near the Pacific coast. So for Japanese people, hoshiimo = Ibaraki.

What is hoshiimo?

Hoshiimo is sweet potato that is steamed, peeled, sliced and dried in the sun. That's all. No sugar, no oil, no salt. The drying makes the potato chewy and very sweet, a little like dried fruit or soft candy.

It's like candy, but healthy. Dangerous.

How is hoshiimo made?

  • Farmers harvest sweet potatoes in autumn, then store them for a few weeks. Resting makes the potatoes sweeter.
  • From around December, they steam the potatoes until soft and peel them while they are still hot.
  • They slice them into thick pieces and lay them on racks outside.
  • The dry, cold wind and winter sunshine of the Ibaraki coast dry them for about one or two weeks.

Winter on the Ibaraki coast is sunny, dry and windy, with little snow. That is perfect weather for drying. This is the big reason why Ibaraki became the hoshiimo capital of Japan.

Types of hoshiimo

  • Hiraboshi (平干し): flat slices. The most common type.
  • Maruboshi (丸干し): a whole small potato, dried round. Thicker, softer and more expensive.
  • Different varieties: Tamayutaka is the classic one, a little firm and gentle. Beniharuka is very sweet and soft. Some farms also sell rare varieties, so try a few and compare!

Sometimes you see white powder on hoshiimo. Don't worry, it is not mold. It is the potato's own sugar coming out to the surface.

How to eat it

  • Just as it is, as a snack. Great with green tea.
  • Warm it a little in a toaster or a frying pan. It gets soft and smells like roasted sweet potato.
  • Keep it in the fridge, or freeze it for a long time. Hoshiimo has no preservatives.

Where to buy hoshiimo in Ibaraki

The best season is from December to spring, when the new hoshiimo comes out. You can find it at roadside stations (michi-no-eki), local supermarkets, farm shops in Hitachinaka and Tokai, and gift shops at Mito Station. Nakaminato Fish Market and Hitachi Seaside Park are also in Hitachinaka, so you can buy hoshiimo on the same day trip.

It is also a perfect omiyage (souvenir). Light, sweet, and very “Ibaraki”.
