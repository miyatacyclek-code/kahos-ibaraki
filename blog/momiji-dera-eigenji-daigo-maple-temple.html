Momiji-dera: the hidden maple temple in Daigo, Ibaraki

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

This week I posted a video and wrote, “This isn’t Kyoto… it’s rural Ibaraki.” I meant it! I have lived in Ibaraki my whole life, but I only just found this temple. It is called Eigenji (永源寺), and local people call it Momiji-dera, the “Maple Temple.”

What is Momiji-dera?

Eigenji is a Zen temple of the Soto school in Daigo town, in the mountains of north Ibaraki. It was founded in 1446. It is also the 7th temple of the Daigo Seven Lucky Gods walk, and it is home to Benzaiten, the goddess of music and the arts.

In autumn, the path up to the temple turns into a tunnel of red and yellow maple leaves. There are maples, zelkova and other trees, so the colors are a little different everywhere you look.

Red leaves above you, the whole town below you.

The view from the top

The temple sits on a hill, so from the grounds you can look down on Daigo town and the mountains around it. When the leaves change, the whole valley is orange and red. I stood there for a long time. No crowds, no noise, just leaves falling.

When to go

  • The leaves usually start to change in late October.
  • The best time is usually early to mid November, and in some years it lasts until late November.
  • Leaves change every year, so check a leaf report before you go.
  • Entry is free, and there is no light-up at night.

How to get there

  • By train: take the JR Suigun Line to Hitachi-Daigo Station. The temple is about a 10-minute walk from the station, up a hill.
  • By car: about 60 minutes from Naka IC on the Joban Expressway. In leaf season there is free temporary parking.
  • Trains on the Suigun Line are not frequent, so check the times before you go.

Make it a full autumn day

Daigo is small, but it has a lot. Fukuroda Falls is just one station away, and the apple orchards are open until about the end of November. So my perfect autumn day is: maple temple in the morning, waterfall at lunch, and sweet Okukuji apples in the afternoon.
