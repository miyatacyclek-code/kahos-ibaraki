Raw egg on rice? Why Japan eats TKG for breakfast

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

In one of my TikToks I had breakfast with a raw egg on rice. Many people in the comments were surprised. Some said "Never!" and some said "I want to try it!" So today I want to explain this very simple Japanese food.

What is TKG?

It is called tamago kake gohan (卵かけご飯). Tamago means egg, kake means poured on, and gohan means rice. Japanese people often just say TKG.

You put hot rice in a bowl, crack a raw egg on top, add a little soy sauce, and mix. That's it. The hot rice warms the egg a little, and it becomes soft and creamy. Some people mix the egg first, some put only the yolk on top. Everyone has their own way.

It is so loved that Japan even has a TKG Day: October 30. It started after the first Tamago Kake Gohan Symposium was held in Unnan city, Shimane, on that day in 2005.

Why is it OK to eat raw eggs in Japan?

In many countries, people are told not to eat raw eggs. In Japan, eggs are washed and checked before they go to shops, and they are kept cold. The date on a Japanese egg pack is special too: it shows how long the egg can be eaten raw. After that date, you can still eat it, but you should cook it well.

So for Japanese people, a fresh egg from the supermarket is a normal breakfast food, not something scary.

Ibaraki and eggs

Here is a fun fact. Ibaraki is one of Japan's biggest egg-producing prefectures. It was number one in Japan for egg production value for 15 years in a row, from 2008 to 2022. Many of the eggs in Tokyo supermarkets come from farms around here.

Tips for visitors

The easiest way to try it is at a beef bowl chain like Sukiya or Yoshinoya. You can order a raw egg on the side for a small price, and put it on your rice or gyudon. Many Japanese hotel breakfasts also have eggs and rice.

A few safety points: do not eat an egg raw if the shell is cracked, and check the date. Small children, pregnant women, elderly people and people with weak immunity are told to avoid raw eggs, even in Japan. And please be careful at home in your country. Eggs there may not be made for eating raw.

Popular TKG toppings

  • Natto (fermented soybeans). Very Ibaraki, because Mito is famous for natto!
  • Chopped green onion and nori seaweed
  • Katsuobushi (bonito flakes)
  • Furikake or a little sesame oil
  • Special TKG soy sauce, sold in Japanese supermarkets

TKG FAQ

  • Is it safe to eat raw eggs in Japan? Fresh Japanese eggs sold for eating raw are handled with strict safety rules. Check the date and don't use cracked eggs.
  • Is the egg cooked by the hot rice? Only a little. It becomes warm and creamy, but it is still mostly raw.
  • Can I eat TKG at a restaurant? Yes. Many beef bowl chains and hotel breakfasts have raw eggs, and some restaurants serve only TKG.
