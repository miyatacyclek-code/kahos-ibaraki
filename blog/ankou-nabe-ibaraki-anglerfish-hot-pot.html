Ankou nabe: Ibaraki’s winter anglerfish hot pot

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Have you seen an anglerfish? Big mouth, sharp teeth, soft floppy body. Not cute! But in Ibaraki, this fish is a winter star. We call it ankou (あんこう), and we eat it in a hot pot called ankou nabe.

When is ankou in season?

Ibaraki Prefecture says the season is November to March. The fish is best in the cold months, so now is the start. There is an old saying in Japan: “Fugu in the west, ankou in the east.” Fugu (pufferfish) is the luxury fish of west Japan, and ankou is the luxury fish of the east.

Scary face, gentle taste. The king of Ibaraki’s winter.

What are the “seven tools”?

Almost every part of the anglerfish can be eaten. People call the parts the “seven tools” (nanatsu dōgu):

  • Flesh
  • Liver
  • Stomach
  • Skin
  • Fins
  • Gills
  • Ovaries

In ankou nabe you can taste all seven in one pot. Each part has a different texture: soft, chewy, a little jelly-like. The broth is often miso or soy sauce based, with vegetables and tofu.

Why do they hang the fish to cut it?

Ankou is about 80% water and very slippery, so it slides on a cutting board. So cooks use “tsurushi-giri”, the hanging cut. They hang the fish on a big hook and cut it in the air. It is a famous Ibaraki skill.

What is dobujiru?

Dobujiru is the fishermen’s version, from Hirakata port in Kitaibaraki. Cooks fry the liver first, then add miso, fish and vegetables. They use little or no water, because the fish and vegetables have so much water inside. The soup becomes thick, orange and very rich. Many people say it is the origin of ankou nabe.

The liver alone, steamed, is called ankimo. Some people call it “foie gras of the sea.”

Where can I try it?

The coast is the place. Oarai and Kitaibaraki are both well known for anglerfish, and many inns and restaurants there serve ankou nabe in winter. Menus and prices change each season, so check with the shop before you go.
