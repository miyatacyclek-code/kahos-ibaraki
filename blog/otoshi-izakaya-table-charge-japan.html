Otoshi: the izakaya dish you didn’t order (and why you pay for it)

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

You sit down at an izakaya in Japan and order a beer. Before you order any food, a small dish arrives. Maybe a little salad, some simmered vegetables, or a few pickles. You did not ask for it. Then, at the end, you see it on the bill.

This is otoshi (お通し). It surprises many visitors, and travelers often ask about it online: is it a mistake, or a scam? It is neither. It is a normal part of izakaya culture.

What is otoshi?

Otoshi is a small starter dish that comes with your first drink. Most izakaya serve it to every guest, and it is added to the bill per person. In Kanto, the area around Tokyo that includes Ibaraki, people call it otoshi. In Kansai, around Osaka and Kyoto, it is usually called tsukidashi (突き出し).

The price is often around 300 to 500 yen per person. Some places charge more, and a few higher-end shops charge over 1,000 yen.

Why does it exist?

There are a few ideas about the name. One common explanation is that otoshi comes from tōsu, “to pass through,” because the dish shows your order has been passed to the kitchen. Tsukidashi means something like “pushed out,” because it can be served right away.

The idea is simple: you get something to eat with your first drink, so you are not waiting with an empty table. Today it also works like a seat charge. In many countries you pay a cover charge or leave a tip. Japan has no tipping, and otoshi is one small way izakaya cover the cost of your seat. The difference is that you get real food for it.

It is also a little surprise. The dish often changes with the season, so it can show what the kitchen is good at.

Tips for visitors

1. Expect it. If you go to an izakaya, think of otoshi as part of the price of sitting down.

2. Ask if you are not sure. Rules are different at each shop. Some will skip it if you tell them before ordering, but many will not, especially if it is part of the seat charge. You can ask, “Otoshi wa arimasu ka?” (Is there otoshi?)

3. Check the menu or website. Many izakaya write the charge on the menu. Big chains and places with English menus often explain it.

4. Enjoy it. It is often a nice homemade dish you would never think to order. Try it before you decide it is a waste of money.

A small dish, a big welcome

Otoshi is not a trick. It is the izakaya saying: sit down, have a drink, here is something to start. Once you know, it feels less like a charge and more like a welcome.
