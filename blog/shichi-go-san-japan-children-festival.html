Shichi-Go-San: why tiny kimono fill Japan’s shrines in November

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

If you visit a shrine in Japan on a weekend in November, you may see something very cute: three-year-olds in tiny kimono, little boys in hakama, and proud parents with cameras. This is Shichi-Go-San (七五三), which means “seven, five, three.”

The official day is November 15. It is not a public holiday, so most families go on a weekend around that date. This year, November 15 is a Sunday, so shrines will be busy.

Why 3, 5 and 7?

Long ago, many children in Japan did not live to grow up. Reaching age three, five or seven was a big deal, so families visited a shrine to say thank you and pray for a healthy future.

The ages come from old ceremonies. At three, children started to grow their hair long. At five, boys wore hakama (formal trousers) for the first time. At seven, girls started wearing a real obi sash, like adults. Today, girls usually celebrate at three and seven, and boys at five. Some families also celebrate boys at three.

Why November 15? A common story is that a Tokugawa shogun held a celebration for his son on this day in the Edo period, and the date stayed.

Not a birthday party. A thank you for making it this far.

The thousand-year candy

Every child gets chitose-ame (千歳飴), “thousand-year candy.” It is a long, thin stick of red and white candy. The long shape is a wish for a long life. The bag usually has cranes and turtles on it, which are symbols of long life in Japan.

Shichi-Go-San in Ibaraki

Today most families keep it simple: a shrine visit, photos at a photo studio, and a nice family meal. But in some parts of Ibaraki, people were known for celebrating in a big way, with parties at hotels or ryokan for relatives, almost like a small wedding reception. A university study even looked at this local custom. These days the parties are getting smaller, but the feeling is the same: the whole family celebrates the child.

Tips for visitors

  • Go to a local shrine on a weekend morning in early or mid November to see families in kimono
  • Do not take close photos of children without asking the parents. Enjoy the moment from a distance
  • Some shops sell chitose-ame in November. It is a fun seasonal snack to try
  • Walk quietly around the main hall. For the families, this is a special day

Shichi-Go-San FAQ

  • When is Shichi-Go-San? The official day is November 15. Many families visit a shrine on a weekend in October or November instead.
  • Is Shichi-Go-San a holiday? No, it is not a public holiday in Japan.
  • Do boys celebrate too? Yes. Boys usually celebrate at age five (in some areas also at three). Girls celebrate at three and seven.
  • Can tourists watch? Yes, you can enjoy the atmosphere at shrines. Just be polite and don't photograph children up close without asking.
