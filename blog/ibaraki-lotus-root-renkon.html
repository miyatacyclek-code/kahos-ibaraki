Ibaraki lotus root: Japan’s renkon capital at Kasumigaura

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Have you ever eaten a vegetable with holes in it? In Japan we call it renkon (れんこん), lotus root. It is crunchy, a little sweet, and when you slice it, it looks like a little flower or a wheel.

And here is something many people don’t know: Ibaraki is the number one lotus root prefecture in Japan.

How much lotus root does Ibaraki grow?

  • In 2020 data, Ibaraki grew about 52% of all lotus root in Japan. That is more than half!
  • Most of it grows in the cities of Tsuchiura and Kasumigaura, next to Lake Kasumigaura, Japan’s second biggest lake.
  • Tsuchiura is called the biggest lotus root producer in Japan, by both field size and amount.

Why does lotus root grow so well here?

The land around Lake Kasumigaura is low, wet and fertile, with lots of water. In the 1970s, when farmers had to make less rice, many changed their wet fields to lotus root. It was a perfect match.

Under the quiet lotus fields, the winter vegetables are hiding in the mud.

When is lotus root in season?

Around Kasumigaura, lotus root is harvested from about July to March, and the peak is autumn. Summer lotus root is clear and juicy. Winter lotus root is white and soft and fluffy. So November is a great time to try it!

How is it harvested?

It is hard work. Farmers go into fields of water and mud about 50 to 60 cm deep, sometimes with water up to their chest. They use a water jet to wash away the mud and find the roots by hand, carefully, so the skin is not damaged. In winter, the fields can be frozen in the morning, so work starts in the afternoon.

How do Japanese people eat lotus root?

  • Kinpira: stir-fried with soy sauce and sugar. A classic side dish.
  • Tempura: crispy outside, crunchy inside.
  • Simmered dishes and soups.
  • Osechi, the New Year food. Lotus root is lucky because you can “see through” the holes into the future.

Where can I try or buy it?

Lotus root is sold at supermarkets and farm stands all over Ibaraki, so it is easy to find. Near Tsuchiura, Yoshida Lotus Root Farm lets you see the lotus fields and buy lotus root products. If you want to buy fresh lotus root there, you need to reserve first, so check their website before you go. The area is also close to the Tsukuba Kasumigaura Ring-Ring Road, so cyclists can enjoy the lake and the fields together.
