Ibaraki sweet potatoes: Japan’s yaki-imo country

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

When the air gets cold in Japan, it is yaki-imo season. Yaki-imo means baked sweet potato, and in Ibaraki it is everywhere: supermarkets, roadside shops, cafes. That makes sense, because Ibaraki is a sweet potato prefecture.

Is Ibaraki really famous for sweet potatoes?

Yes! According to Ibaraki Prefecture, Ibaraki is No.1 in Japan for the farm output value of sweet potatoes, and No.2 for growing area and harvest amount. Kagoshima is the other big one.

The main areas are Namegata and Hokota, near Lake Kasumigaura and the Pacific coast. The land is mild and the soil drains water well, which sweet potatoes like. Around 1945, this area had the largest sweet potato fields in Japan.

What is Namegata Kansho?

Namegata Kansho (Namegata sweet potato) got a GI mark in 2023. GI is Japan’s official label for special local products, like Champagne in France. It covers Namegata, Itako, Kashima, Hokota, Omitama and Kasumigaura.

Why are they so sweet?

After the harvest, farmers “cure” the potatoes and keep them at a steady temperature for a while. Slowly, the starch turns into sugar. So a sweet potato in late autumn and winter is often sweeter than one fresh from the field.

Patience makes a sweet potato sweeter.

Which kinds should I try?

  • Beni haruka: very sweet and soft, almost like cream when baked
  • Silk sweet: smooth and silky, as the name says
  • Beni azuma: a classic, a bit more fluffy and chestnut-like
  • Beni masari: moist and sweet, another Namegata favorite

Where can I eat yaki-imo in Ibaraki?

  • Rapoppo Namegata Farmer’s Village in Namegata, a sweet potato theme park with a potato cafe
  • Tsubo Yakiimo Dojo in Kasama, where potatoes are baked standing up in big clay pots
  • Any supermarket in winter: look for the warm yaki-imo corner near the entrance

Check opening days before you go, because many small shops close one or two days a week. And if you like chewy snacks, try hoshiimo (dried sweet potato), Ibaraki’s other winter treasure.
