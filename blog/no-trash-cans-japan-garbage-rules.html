No trash cans in Japan? Where the garbage goes

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

It is one of the most common surprises for people visiting Japan. You finish a drink, look around for a trash can… and there isn’t one. Not on the street, not in the park, sometimes not even at the station. But the streets are still clean. How?

Why there are so few trash cans

There are a few reasons. After the sarin gas attack on the Tokyo subway in 1995, many trash cans in stations and public places were closed or removed for safety. Some places had already removed them before that, for other reasons like smells, crows or animals. And after they were gone, many never came back.

The bigger reason is a habit. In Japan, most people simply carry their trash home. It is not a written law. It is just what everyone does, and children learn it from a young age.

At home: the sorting rules

At home, garbage is a serious job. Every city has its own rules, and they can be very different, even between two towns next to each other. A typical list looks like this:

  • Burnable garbage (food waste, paper, small plastic things)
  • Non-burnable garbage (broken glass, small metal things)
  • Recyclables: cans, glass bottles and PET bottles, often on different days
  • Big items like furniture, which you must book and sometimes pay for

Many cities also sell special garbage bags. For example, Tsuchiura city in Ibaraki sells official paid bags in local shops. If you use the wrong bag or put it out on the wrong day, it may stay there with a sticker on it.

The countryside version

In small towns, each neighborhood has a gomi station, a little spot where everyone puts their bags on collection morning. Often there is a net or a box to stop crows. Neighbors notice who follows the rules, so getting it right is a quiet way to show you are a good member of the community.

Tips for visitors

  • Bring a small plastic bag in your backpack for your trash.
  • Many convenience stores have bins, but they are mainly for things you bought there.
  • Look next to vending machines. There is often a bin just for cans and bottles.
  • At stations, you may find bins near the ticket gates or on the platform.

So the secret of clean streets in Japan is not more trash cans. It is millions of people carrying a little bag home.
