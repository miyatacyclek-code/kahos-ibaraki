Ibaraki Airport: how to get to Tokyo, Mito and more

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Some people fly to Japan and land in Ibaraki, not Tokyo. Ibaraki Airport is in Omitama City, in the middle of the prefecture. It is small and quiet, so you get out fast. But the big question is always: how do I get from here to Tokyo, or to Mito?

Where can you fly from Ibaraki Airport?

Most flights are Skymark domestic flights. In the 2026 summer timetable, the routes were Sapporo (New Chitose), Kobe, Fukuoka and Naha in Okinawa. There is also an international route to Cheongju in South Korea. Routes change, so please check the airport website before you book.

How to get from Ibaraki Airport to Tokyo Station

There is a direct bus to Tokyo Station. It takes about 100 minutes. The official site lists the fare as ¥2,200 for adults and ¥1,100 for children. You need to reserve a seat in advance (online, from one month before). In Tokyo, the bus uses the Yaesu South Exit highway bus area. There are not many buses a day, so check the timetable first.

Small airport, big shortcut into the real Japan.

How to get to Mito, Ishioka or Tsukuba

  • Mito Station: express highway bus, about 40 minutes, ¥1,500 for adults. A slower local bus is cheaper.
  • Ishioka Station (JR Joban Line): about 35 minutes, ¥680 for adults. From Ishioka you can take a train to Tokyo.
  • Tsukuba: buses leave from bus stop 4, for people going on to the Tsukuba Express.
  • Taxi: about ¥7,000 to Mito Station (45 minutes), or about ¥5,500 to Ishioka Station (30 minutes).

You can pay most buses with cash or an IC card like Suica.

Is parking free at Ibaraki Airport?

Yes! Parking is free, with about 3,600 spaces, from 5:30 to 22:00. That is rare for an airport in Japan. It can get crowded on busy days, so come early.

What can you do at the airport?

Go up to the send-off deck on the 2nd floor and watch planes take off very close. Local families come just for this. Inside, there are restaurants, a shop with Ibaraki goods, and even a Seicomart konbini. Free Wi-Fi is there too.

If you land in Ibaraki, don’t rush to Tokyo. Stay one night and eat some natto! Tell me on YouTube where you flew in from, or share your trip with #KahoIbaraki.
