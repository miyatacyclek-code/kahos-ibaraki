Ushiku Daibutsu: Ibaraki’s giant 120m Buddha

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Driving in southern Ibaraki, you suddenly see a huge Buddha standing above the trees. It looks almost too big to be real. This is Ushiku Daibutsu, in Ushiku City, and it is one of the biggest statues in the world.

How big is Ushiku Daibutsu?

It is 120 meters tall in total. The Buddha itself is 100 meters, and it stands on a 10 meter base and a 10 meter lotus flower. It is made of bronze sheets on a steel frame, and it was finished in 1993. From 1993 to 2008 it was the tallest statue in the world.

The famous Great Buddha of Nara is about 15 meters tall. People say it could fit in the palm of Ushiku Daibutsu’s hand!

Nara’s Great Buddha could sit in the palm of its hand.

Who is the Buddha?

The statue is Amitabha Buddha (Amida). It was built by the Jodo Shinshu (True Pure Land) school of Buddhism, to remember the birth of Shinran, the founder of the school.

Can you go inside?

Yes, and this is the best part. Inside there is a small museum on several floors:

  • A dark hall with light and incense at the start
  • A space for copying Buddhist sutras (extra fee)
  • A wall with thousands of small golden Buddha statues
  • An observation floor at 85 meters, with windows looking over the Kanto Plain

When is the best time to visit?

The park around the statue changes with the seasons. Cherry blossoms usually bloom in early to mid April, pink moss phlox in April, poppies in May, and cosmos in September and October. In winter the air is clear, so the view from 85 meters can be very nice. From October to February, it is open 9:30 to 16:30, and last entry is 30 minutes before closing.

How to get there from Tokyo

Take the JR Joban Line to Ushiku Station. It is about one hour from Ueno or Shinagawa. From the east exit, take a bus to the Daibutsu, about 20 to 30 minutes. Buses are not very frequent, especially on weekdays, so check the time table before you go. A taxi is also an option.
