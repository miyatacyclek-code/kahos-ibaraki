Oarai Isosaki Shrine: Ibaraki’s torii in the sea

by Kaho · Ibaraki, Japan

Have you seen a photo of a torii gate standing on rocks in the sea, with big waves and the sun coming up behind it? That place is in Ibaraki. It is the Kamiiso no Torii (神磯の鳥居) of Oarai Isosaki Shrine (大洗磯前神社), in the beach town of Oarai.

What is Oarai Isosaki Shrine?

  • It was founded in 856, more than 1,100 years ago.
  • It is for two gods, Onamuchi and Sukunahikona, who are said to have helped make the land of Japan.
  • The old shrine was destroyed in war in the 1500s. It was rebuilt in 1690 with help from Tokugawa Mitsukuni, the famous lord of Mito.
  • Look for the frog statues. In Japanese, “kaeru” means both “frog” and “to come back home safely.”

The legend of the torii on the rocks

The story says that in 856, the two gods came down onto a rock by the sea here. People built the shrine on the land side of that rock. Today a torii stands on the rock, so the gods can come and go from the ocean.

The sun comes up out of the Pacific, right behind the gate.

When is the best time to visit?

Go at sunrise. The torii faces east, so the morning sun comes up over the sea behind it. On New Year’s Day, the first sunrise of the year rises between the pillars, and many people and photographers come to see it. So it is very crowded then. On a normal winter morning it is much calmer, and the air is often clear. The torii also looks beautiful in the moonlight.

Safety tips

Please do not climb on the rocks. They are slippery, the waves are strong, especially at high tide, and there have been deadly accidents. Watch from the shore.

How to get there

  • By train: go to Mito Station, then take the Oarai-Kashima Line to Oarai Station. From there it is about 10 minutes by bus.
  • By bus from Mito: buses from Mito Station bound for Oarai stop at “Oarai Jinja-mae” (大洗神社前). It takes about 35 minutes.
  • By car: about 5.5 km from Mito-Oarai IC. The shrine has free parking for about 70 cars.

Oarai is also a great town for seafood, so you can visit the shrine in the morning and have lunch by the port.
